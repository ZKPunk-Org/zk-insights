This week we cover anonymous credentials from standardized signatures, bitwidth-aware SNARKs, Bulletproofs++ range proofs, Reed-Solomon polynomial commitments, and under-constrained circuit diagnostics. We also look at zkVM memory management, AI-assisted auditing and formal verification, post-quantum engineering discussions, and the research path from Nova to Magnetar.

## Blogs

### zkVM: Proof-based general-purpose computing with Magnetar
- Srinath Setty, Microsoft Research
- Introduces Magnetar, a RISC-V zkVM built on standard hash functions and binary-field arithmetic, tracing its design through Spartan, memory-checking arguments, and BinarySpartan. Reports average speedups of 10–39× over the compared zkVMs across 15 benchmarks on the same machine, with proof verification around 12 ms. Explains how efficient verification enables recursive composition for long computations.
- <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/proof-based-general-purpose-computing-with-magnetar/>

### Lambda VM: continuations
- Aligned Layer
- Compares how RISC Zero, OpenVM, SP1, and ZisK split long executions, then explores two approaches to bounding Lambda VM's prover memory. Epochs use local proofs and a global memory-consistency argument; prove-and-retire rebuilds tables across passes to trade computation for lower memory use. Epochs are implemented, while prove-and-retire remains experimental.
- <https://blog.alignedlayer.com/lambda-vm-continuations/>

### Auditing in the age of (good enough) AI
- Fredrik Dahlgren, Trail of Bits
- Describes building a decompiler, static analyses, and a Lean execution model to support a Miden zkVM audit. The tools exposed an under-constrained advice value that could enable Falcon signature forgery. AI-assisted formalization also produced 95 correctness proofs covering the core library's binary arithmetic components and helped identify two further implementation bugs.
- <https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/09/18/auditing-in-the-age-of-good-enough-ai/>

### Better Codes - and how they can be worse if you're not careful
- Kobi Gurkan, zkSecurity
- Examines an AI-assisted formalization failure in better.codes: when independent Lean replay struggled with an extension-field irreducibility certificate, several agents proposed disabling the replay check. Explains how that bypass breaks the verification boundary and how making the field cardinality explicit resolves the underlying type-equivalence problem without removing the check.
- <https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/better-codes/>

### ethlambda at the Cambridge workshops: post-quantum signatures, fast finality, and formal verification
- LambdaClass
- Reports technical discussions on post-quantum signature aggregation, hash-function proving costs, and matching Lean specifications to deployed binaries. Also highlights open questions about the security models used for recursive proofs and Fiat-Shamir, alongside experience scaling leanConsensus devnets from 32 to 4,096 validators.
- <https://blog.lambdaclass.com/ethlambda-at-the-cambridge-workshops-post-quantum-signatures-fast-finality-and-formal-verification/>

## Papers

### Faster and Smaller Anonymous Credentials from Standardized Signatures
- Chad Sharp, Paul Grubbs
- Recommend: 🌟
- Introduces GordieHowe, combining multi-field ZK proofs for ECDSA-, RSA-, and ML-DSA-based credentials with Lean-certified compiler optimizations and machine-checked proof-system security theorems. Reports an ECDSA-signed mobile driving license presentation in 64.8 ms on four cores with a 276 KiB proof. Extends BCS to series-parallel IOPs, analyzing knowledge soundness, zero knowledge, and a form of simulation extractability in the QROM. First submitted October 23 and revised October 25.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2615>

### Mikros: Bitwidth-Aware SNARKs over Extensions of Small Fields
- Mingshu CONG
- Packs small-field vectors into extension-field elements to make inner-product proofs, Hadamard-product proofs, polynomial commitments, and permutation arguments bitwidth-aware without requiring binary fields. On batches of 256 SHA-256 compressions, Keccak permutations, and BLAKE3 compressions, reports prover speedups of 2.19×, 2.55×, and 1.85× over Flock, respectively. Submitted October 26.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2646>

### Notes on BulletProofs++ Range Proofs
- Philipp Jovanovic, Arnab Roy
- Recommend: 🌟
- Gives a self-contained specification of 64-bit and general batched range proofs, with honest-verifier zero knowledge and exact-form knowledge soundness. Its Rust implementation over Ristretto255 reports proving and verification speedups of up to 7.8× and 4.5× over regular Bulletproofs. Also corrects two formulas in the original Bulletproofs++ paper. Submitted October 26.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2648>

### ReedWeave: Faster Reed-Solomon Polynomial Commitments from Interleaving and Folding
- Yuhao Jia, Zhe Li, Chaoping Xing, Yizhou Yao, Chen Yuan, Jielong Zhang
- Combines interleaved Reed-Solomon encoding with an initial higher-arity fold to reduce early prover work while retaining polylogarithmic verification. Its Rust benchmark over Goldilocks uses 32 threads and targets 100-bit security; at degree 2^24, the reported proof is 594.7 KiB, smaller than FRI's but larger than STIR's. First submitted September 22 and revised October 29.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2147>

### Jacobian Diagnostics for Under-Constrained Zero-Knowledge Circuits
- Vijaykumar Singh
- Uses exact Jacobian linear algebra to detect and localize potential freedom in security-relevant circuit wires, with experiments on R1CS and PLONKish standard-gate systems. Separates local infinitesimal freedom from finite-field non-uniqueness and provides certificates under stated conditions. Shows why honest witnesses can miss defects exposed by adversarial witnesses, positioning the method as a candidate detector paired with confirmation. First submitted September 1 and revised October 26.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1852>
