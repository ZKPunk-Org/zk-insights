This week we cover experiments in post-quantum signature aggregation for Ethereum, a machine-checked GKR soundness bound, and a conversation on cryptographic protocols designed by AI. The papers explore Reed–Solomon proximity gaps, zkTLS, and post-quantum Bitcoin bridges.

## Blogs

### EIP-8288 Benchmarks for Post-Quantum Signatures and Proof Propagation
- Manuel B. Santos, Csaba Kiraly
- One post benchmarks leanVM proofs of signature verification for five post-quantum schemes. The other simulates proof propagation in a mempool with recursive aggregation. In a 1,000-node simulation at two signature transactions per second, fetching proofs on demand reduced per-node upload from 10.5 to 2.4 Mbit/s.
- <https://ethresear.ch/t/exploring-post-quantum-signature-aggregation-for-eip-8288/26261>
- <https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263>

### GKR with a Machine-Checked Core, and a Sparse Prover Made Faster with Byte-Identical Proofs
- StateSync-GKR
- Describes a GKR soundness bound checked in Isabelle/HOL and its application to sparse Merkle updates. The authors report that the Rust prover runs 2.41–2.71× faster while producing byte-identical proofs.
- <https://ethresear.ch/t/gkr-with-a-machine-checked-core-and-a-sparse-prover-made-faster-with-byte-identical-proofs/26226>

## Papers

### LEASH: When LaBRADOR’s Guarantees Go Astray
- Xiang Wang, Ji Xiang
- Shows that LaBRADOR's parameter selection can violate completeness even for valid binary R1CS witnesses. Identifies gaps in the recursive relation transformations and knowledge extraction analysis, and proposes repairs.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2898>

### Affine Combiners for Reed-Solomon Proximity Gaps
- Alessandro Chiesa, Guy Weissenberg, Eylon Yogev, Kai Zhe Zheng
- Tightens Reed–Solomon proximity gap error bounds with affine combiners over arbitrary evaluation domains, including binary extension fields. The agreement threshold can approach the Johnson threshold with the loss of one agreement coordinate.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2863>

### Rethinking Zero-Knowledge TLS: Proof of Protocol Execution and the Reclaim Protocol
- Andrey Bozhko, Kirill Kutsenok
- Defines zkPoPE, an application-layer model for proving properties of private data from an authentic protocol execution. The authors show that Reclaim realizes the zkPoPE functionality in the UC framework under its stated trust model and report attesting a 1.2 MB response in 7.28 seconds on a phone.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2187>

### Toward Efficient Post-Quantum Bitcoin Bridges
- Stephen Duan, Vanhger Pham, Blake Hou, Ethan Zhu, Jingping Yin
- Garbles Ziren's WHIR-based STARK verifier for BitVM-style verification. The prototype measures the tradeoff between prover time, off-chain storage, and on-chain dispute data, with one benchmark requiring 2.81 GB of garbled tables and 3.93 million virtual bytes on-chain.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2877>

### Twisted Towers: Fully Costed Special Tower NFS Attacks on BLS12-381 and BN254
- Gautam Botrel
- Corrects the earlier BLS12-381 analysis and adds a BN254 estimate. The heuristic cost estimates for computing discrete logarithms in the respective pairing target groups are $2^{127.5}$ and $2^{103.2}$ machine instructions, excluding storage costs.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2831>

### The Geometry of Zero Knowledge: Tight Masking and Efficient Multilinear Arguments
- Xiang Wang, Ji Xiang
- Builds masking polynomials around what the verifier queries. On matched eight-column BLS12-381 benchmarks, the authors report that query-aware masking saves 4,128 proof bytes and speeds up proving by 1.40–1.67×.
- <https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2019>
